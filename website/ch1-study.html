Section 01
Chemical Reactions
🔥 Why does food cook? Why does iron rust? Why do we breathe? — all chemical reactions happening around you every second.

A chemical reaction is a process where one or more substances called reactants are converted into one or more new substances called products, which have entirely different properties. A key requirement: new substances must be formed.

The key law governing all chemical reactions is the Law of Conservation of Mass (Antoine Lavoisier, 1774): "Mass can neither be created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction." Total mass of reactants = Total mass of products, always.

🔍 5 Signs a Chemical Reaction Has Occurred

🎨
Change in Colour
The colour of one or more substances changes.
Iron nail in CuSO₄ — solution turns blue to green
💨
Gas Evolution
A gas is produced — seen as bubbles or detected by smell.
Zn + H₂SO₄ → H₂ bubbles form
🧊
Precipitate Formed
An insoluble solid settles out from solution.
AgNO₃ + NaCl → white AgCl↓
🌡️
Temperature Change
Heat released (exothermic) or absorbed (endothermic).
CaO + H₂O → flask becomes very hot
💡
Change in State
A solid, liquid, or gas may form that wasn't there before.
Mg (solid) burns → MgO (white powder)

⚖️ Physical Change vs Chemical Change

Physical ChangeChemical Change
No new substance formedNew substance(s) always formed
Change in physical properties onlyChange in chemical properties/composition
Usually reversibleUsually irreversible
No energy change (or very little)Energy absorbed or released
Melting ice, stretching rubberBurning wood, rusting iron, souring milk
⚠️
Common Mistake
Dissolving salt in water looks like a chemical change — but no new substance forms, and you can recover the salt by evaporating the water. It is a physical change.
✅ Concept Check
Q1. Name 3 signs that indicate a chemical reaction when Zn is added to dilute H₂SO₄.
👁 Show Answer
1. Gas evolution (H₂ bubbles — burns with pop sound). 2. Temperature change (test tube becomes warm — exothermic). 3. Zinc gradually dissolves (change in state/appearance).
Q2. Is melting of wax a physical or chemical change? Justify.
👁 Show Answer
Physical change. Wax changes state (solid → liquid) but no new substance forms. On cooling, it solidifies back — reversible. Therefore it is a physical change, not chemical.
Section 02
Writing Chemical Equations
💬 Describing a reaction in words takes a whole paragraph. A chemical equation says the same thing in one line.

A chemical equation is a shorthand representation using symbols and formulae. Reactants are written on the left, products on the right, separated by an arrow (→). The arrow means "reacts to give".

From Word to Symbol — 4 Steps

1
Write the Word Equation
Magnesium + Oxygen → Magnesium oxide
2
Replace with Chemical Formulae
Mg + O₂ → MgO   (unbalanced — check atom count)
3
Balance the Equation
2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO   (left: 2 Mg, 2 O | right: 2 Mg, 2 O ✓)
4
Add State Symbols — most complete form
2Mg(s) + O₂(g) → 2MgO(s)

What the Symbols Mean

SymbolMeaningExample
(s)SolidFe(s)
(l)LiquidH₂O(l)
(g)GasCO₂(g)
(aq)Aqueous solutionNaCl(aq)
↑Gas evolved / escapingH₂↑
↓Precipitate formedBaSO₄↓
→ (above/below)Reaction condition→(heat) or →(MnO₂)
📋
What a Balanced Equation Tells You
✔ Identities (formulae) of reactants and products  |  ✔ Physical states of all substances  |  ✔ Molar ratio in which they react  |  ✔ Reaction conditions (heat, catalyst, light)  |  ✘ Does NOT tell you rate of reaction, feasibility, or concentration.
Section 03
Balancing Chemical Equations
⚖️ Atoms can't appear or disappear. Whatever enters a reaction must come out — just rearranged.

We balance equations because of the Law of Conservation of Mass. The number of atoms of every element must be the same on both sides.

💡
The Golden Rule
You can ONLY change coefficients (numbers in front). NEVER change subscripts inside formulae — that changes the substance itself. H₂O is water. H₂O₂ is hydrogen peroxide. Very different things.

Worked Example — 3Fe + 4H₂O → Fe₃O₄ + 4H₂

Step 1 — Unbalanced
Fe + H₂O → Fe₃O₄ + H₂
Step 2 — Balance Fe first: put 3Fe on left
3Fe + H₂O → Fe₃O₄ + H₂
Step 3 — Balance O: need 4 O on left → 4H₂O
3Fe + 4H₂O → Fe₃O₄ + H₂
Step 4 — Balance H: 4H₂O = 8H, so put 4H₂
3Fe + 4H₂O → Fe₃O₄ + 4H₂
✅
Verify
Left: 3 Fe, 8 H, 4 O  |  Right: 3 Fe (in Fe₃O₄), 4 O (in Fe₃O₄), 8 H (in 4H₂)  |  ✅ Balanced!
🎮 Practice Balancer
Fill in coefficients. Leave blank or enter 1 for coefficient of 1. Press Check.
✅ Concept Check
Q. Why can't we balance an equation by changing the subscripts in a formula?
👁 Show Answer
Subscripts define the substance itself. Changing H₂O to H₂O₂ changes water into hydrogen peroxide — a completely different compound. We can only change the number of molecules (coefficients), not what the molecules are.
Section 04
Types of Chemical Reactions
🗂️ Thousands of reactions exist — but they all follow just a handful of patterns. Learn the pattern, recognise any reaction.
➕
Combination
A + B → AB
Two substances combine into a single product.
2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO
🧠 "combine" = join into ONE
💥
Decomposition
AB → A + B
One compound breaks into simpler substances.
CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂
🧠 "de-compose" = fall into MANY
🔄
Displacement
A + BC → AC + B
More reactive element kicks out less reactive one.
Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu
🧠 "displace" = take someone's seat
🔀
Double Displacement
AB + CD → AD + CB
Two ionic compounds swap their ions.
Na₂SO₄ + BaCl₂ → BaSO₄↓ + 2NaCl
🧠 "double" = both partners swap
🔁
Oxidation-Reduction
Redox — always simultaneous
One substance gains oxygen (oxidised), another loses (reduced).
CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O
🧠 OIL RIG — always together
Section 04a
Combination Reactions

Two or more reactants combine to form a single product. Many combination reactions are also exothermic.

Mg burning in air
2Mg(s) + O₂(g) → 2MgO(s)
Dazzling white flame; white ash. Exothermic.
Quicklime + Water (Slaking)
CaO(s) + H₂O(l) → Ca(OH)₂(aq) + Heat
Flask becomes very hot. Exothermic combination reaction.
Haber Process
N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) →high T, P, Fe 2NH₃(g)
📋
Board Tip
CaO + H₂O is the most asked combination reaction. Questions often say "Name the type AND state whether it is exothermic or endothermic." Answer: Combination + Exothermic.
Section 04b
Decomposition Reactions
🔬 Combination and decomposition are opposites. If one is marriage, the other is divorce.

A single compound breaks into two or more simpler substances. Energy (heat, electricity, or light) is usually needed. There are three types based on energy source.

🔥 1. Thermal Decomposition — Heat

Limestone decomposition
CaCO₃(s) →heat CaO(s) + CO₂(g)↑
Used industrially to make quicklime for cement.
Lead nitrate decomposition
2Pb(NO₃)₂(s) →heat 2PbO(s) + 4NO₂(g)↑ + O₂(g)↑
Brown fumes of NO₂; yellow residue of PbO. Classic board observation Q.
Ferrous sulphate decomposition
2FeSO₄(s) →heat Fe₂O₃(s) + SO₂(g)↑ + SO₃(g)↑
Green crystals → white (loss of water) → brown-black residue. Pungent smell of SO₂.

⚡ 2. Electrolytic Decomposition — Electricity

Electrolysis of water
2H₂O(l) →electricity 2H₂(g)↑ + O₂(g)↑
H₂ at cathode; O₂ at anode. Volume ratio H₂:O₂ = 2:1.
💡
Why 2:1 ratio?
The equation produces 2 moles H₂ for every 1 mole O₂. By Avogadro's law, equal moles of gas at same T and P occupy equal volumes — so volume ratio = mole ratio = 2:1.

☀️ 3. Photochemical Decomposition — Light

Silver chloride in sunlight
2AgCl(s) →sunlight 2Ag(s) + Cl₂(g)↑
White AgCl → grey/black. Ag metal forms. Used in photography.
Silver bromide in sunlight
2AgBr(s) →sunlight 2Ag(s) + Br₂(g)↑
Used in black-and-white photographic film.

Section 04c
Displacement Reactions
🪑 A more popular kid walks in and takes a less popular kid's cafeteria seat. That's displacement in chemistry.

A displacement reaction occurs when a more reactive element displaces a less reactive element from its salt solution. Pattern: A + BC → AC + B (A is more reactive than B).

Iron displaces copper
Fe(s) + CuSO₄(aq) → FeSO₄(aq) + Cu(s)
Blue CuSO₄ → light green FeSO₄. Reddish-brown Cu deposits on iron nail.
Zinc displaces hydrogen from acid
Zn(s) + H₂SO₄(aq) → ZnSO₄(aq) + H₂(g)↑

📊 Reactivity Series (top = most reactive)

A metal can only displace metals below it.

⚠️
Classic Mistake
Students write Cu + FeSO₄ → CuSO₄ + Fe. This is WRONG. Copper is LESS reactive than iron. A less reactive element cannot displace a more reactive one. Always check the reactivity series first.
Section 04d
Double Displacement Reactions

Two ionic compounds in solution exchange their ions. Pattern: AB + CD → AD + CB. Usually forms a precipitate, water, or a gas.

Precipitation — white precipitate
Na₂SO₄(aq) + BaCl₂(aq) → BaSO₄(s)↓ + 2NaCl(aq)
Golden Rain — yellow precipitate
2KI(aq) + Pb(NO₃)₂(aq) → PbI₂(s)↓ + 2KNO₃(aq)
Bright yellow lead iodide precipitate — "Golden Rain" demonstration.
Neutralisation — forms water
NaOH(aq) + HCl(aq) → NaCl(aq) + H₂O(l)

Section 05
Exothermic & Endothermic Reactions
🌡️ Every chemical reaction either releases energy to its surroundings or absorbs energy from them.
🔥 Exothermic
Releases heat. Temp rises.
• C + O₂ → CO₂ + Heat
• CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ + Heat
• Respiration
• Any combustion reaction
❄️ Endothermic
Absorbs heat. Temp drops.
• CaCO₃ →(Δ) CaO + CO₂
• 2H₂O →(elec) 2H₂ + O₂
• Photosynthesis
• Dissolving NH₄Cl in water
📋
Board Favourite — Respiration vs Photosynthesis
Respiration = Exothermic (releases energy from glucose for body functions).
Photosynthesis = Endothermic (absorbs light energy from the sun to make glucose).
These are asked together almost every year.
✅ Concept Check
Q. Classify: (a) Burning LPG  (b) Dissolving NH₄Cl in water  (c) Sublimation of dry ice  (d) CaO + H₂O
👁 Show Answer
(a) Exothermic — combustion releases heat.
(b) Endothermic — flask becomes cold (NH₄Cl absorbs heat).
(c) Endothermic — dry ice absorbs heat to sublimate.
(d) Exothermic — CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ + Heat. The mixture gets very hot.
Section 06
Oxidation & Reduction
⚗️ These two always come in pairs. Like a seesaw — one goes up, the other goes down. You can't have one without the other.
OIL
OXIDATION
Gain of oxygen
OR loss of hydrogen
RIG
REDUCTION
Loss of oxygen
OR gain of hydrogen
🧠
OIL RIG Mnemonic
Oxidation Is Loss  |  Reduction Is Gain. Write this down and never forget it.

Worked Example — CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O

CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O
CuO loses oxygen → CuO is REDUCED → CuO = Oxidising Agent
H₂ gains oxygen → H₂ is OXIDISED → H₂ = Reducing Agent
TermDefinitionExample
OxidationGain of O₂ or loss of H₂Mg → MgO (gains O)
ReductionLoss of O₂ or gain of H₂CuO → Cu (loses O)
Oxidising AgentCauses oxidation; itself gets reducedCuO in CuO+H₂
Reducing AgentCauses reduction; itself gets oxidisedH₂ in CuO+H₂
Redox ReactionOxidation + reduction simultaneouslyFe₂O₃ + 2Al → Al₂O₃ + 2Fe

🃏 Identify OA and RA — Flip Cards

Oxidised? Reduced?
Fe₂O₃ + 2Al →
Al₂O₃ + 2Fe
Al = Reducing Agent
Al gains O → oxidised. Fe₂O₃ loses O → reduced. Fe₂O₃ = OA.
Oxidised? Reduced?
H₂S + I₂ → 2HI + S
H₂S = Reducing Agent
H₂S loses H → oxidised. I₂ gains H → reduced. I₂ = OA.
Oxidised? Reduced?
2PbO + C → 2Pb + CO₂
C = Reducing Agent
C gains O → oxidised. PbO loses O → reduced. PbO = OA.
⚠️
Students Always Get This Backwards
The Reducing Agent gets oxidised. The Oxidising Agent gets reduced. The agent does TO others what its name says — and gets the opposite done TO itself. This is the most common error in redox questions.
Section 07
Corrosion
🏗️ Corrosion destroys infrastructure worth crores every year. From bridges to ships — it's slow chemistry with massive consequences.

Corrosion is the slow chemical deterioration of metals when they react with atmospheric oxygen, moisture, CO₂, or acids. It is a type of oxidation reaction. The most familiar example is rusting of iron.

Rusting of Iron
4Fe(s) + 3O₂(g) + xH₂O(l) → 2Fe₂O₃·xH₂O(s)
Rust = Hydrated ferric oxide. Brown-red flaky substance.
Tarnishing of Silver
4Ag + 2H₂S + O₂ → 2Ag₂S + 2H₂O
Black Ag₂S (silver sulphide) forms on silver articles.
Greening of Copper
Cu + CO₂ + O₂ + H₂O → Cu(OH)₂·CuCO₃
Green basic copper carbonate on copper statues and coins.

🛡️ Prevention of Corrosion

🎨
Painting
Physical barrier between metal and atmospheric O₂/moisture.
⚙️
Oil / Grease
Prevents moisture contact. Used for machine parts.
🔩
Galvanisation
Zinc coating on iron — sacrificial protection.
🧪
Alloying
Stainless steel (Fe+Cr+Ni) is rust-resistant.
⚡
Electroplating
Chromium or tin coating via electrolysis.
💡
Why Galvanisation Works Even When Scratched
Zinc is more reactive than iron. Even when the zinc coating is scratched, zinc still corrodes preferentially — protecting the iron underneath. This is called sacrificial protection.
Section 08
Rancidity
🍟 Ever smelled old oil or chips that taste bitter and off? That's rancidity — slow oxidation turning your food bad.

Rancidity is the slow oxidation of fats and oils in food by atmospheric oxygen. The oxidised products (peroxides, aldehydes) develop an unpleasant smell and taste, making food unfit to eat. It is essentially corrosion of food.

🛡️ Prevention of Rancidity

1
Antioxidants
BHA or Vitamin C are added — they get oxidised first, protecting the food fats.
2
Airtight Containers / Nitrogen Flushing
Removing or replacing oxygen with inert N₂ gas prevents oxidation. Chips are packed in N₂ for this reason.
3
Refrigeration
Low temperature slows the rate of oxidation, delaying rancidity.
4
Vacuum Packing
Removing all air (including O₂) from packaging completely prevents oxidation.
