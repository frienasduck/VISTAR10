Chapter Overview

Chapter 3 is one of the most consistently tested chapters in Class 10 boards — carrying 7–10 marks every year. It covers physical and chemical properties of metals and non-metals, the reactivity series, ionic bonding, full metallurgy (ore → refined metal), corrosion, and alloys. Mastering extraction processes, all key equations, and the reactivity series is essential for scoring full marks.

🔧
Physical Properties
Metals: shiny, malleable, ductile, conductors, high m.p. Non-metals: dull, brittle, insulators, low m.p. Exceptions: Hg (liquid metal), Graphite (conducts), Iodine (lustrous), Diamond (hard non-metal).
⚡
Chemical Properties
Reactions with O₂, H₂O, dilute acids. Metals → basic oxides. Non-metals → acidic oxides. Exceptions: Al₂O₃, ZnO = amphoteric. Na/K react violently with cold water. Cu, Ag, Au — no reaction with acid.
📊
Reactivity Series
K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > Zn > Fe > Pb > (H) > Cu > Ag > Au > Pt. Determines extraction method, displacement reactions, reaction with water and acids. Core of entire chapter.
🔗
Ionic Bonding
Complete transfer of electrons. Metal → cation (+). Non-metal → anion (−). Electrostatic attraction = ionic bond. High m.p., conducts in molten/solution, soluble in water, insoluble in organic solvents.
🏭
Metallurgy
4 steps: Concentration → Oxide formation (roasting/calcination) → Reduction (C or electrolysis) → Refining. Blast furnace (Fe), Hall-Héroult (Al), Downs cell (Na). Electrolytic refining for Cu.
🌧️
Corrosion
Rusting = Fe + O₂ + H₂O → Fe₂O₃·xH₂O. Rust: porous, flaky, not protective. Al₂O₃: hard, impervious, protective (passivation). Prevention: galvanisation, painting, alloying, electroplating, sacrificial protection.
⚗️
Alloys
Homogeneous mixture of metals (or metal + non-metal). Brass (Cu+Zn), Bronze (Cu+Sn), Steel (Fe+C), Stainless steel (Fe+Cr+Ni), Solder (Pb+Sn), Duralumin (Al+Cu+Mg+Mn), Amalgam (Hg+Ag+Sn).
🪨
Ores & Minerals
Ore = mineral from which metal extracted profitably. Gangue = impurities. Flux removes gangue → slag. Important ores: Bauxite (Al), Haematite (Fe), ZnS (Zn), Galena (Pb), Cinnabar (Hg), Rock salt (Na).
⚗️ Must-Know Equations
Fe + Steam
3Fe + 4H₂O(steam) → Fe₃O₄ + 4H₂↑  |  Ca + 2H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ + H₂↑ (milky)
Displacement
Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu  |  Fe₂O₃ + 2Al → Al₂O₃ + 2Fe (Thermite)
Blast Furnace
Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂  |  CaO + SiO₂ → CaSiO₃ (slag)
Roasting
2ZnS + 3O₂ → 2ZnO + 2SO₂↑  |  ZnO + C → Zn + CO↑
Amphoteric
Al₂O₃ + 6HCl → 2AlCl₃ + 3H₂O  |  Al₂O₃ + 2NaOH → 2NaAlO₂ + H₂O
Electrolysis
Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Al (cathode)  |  2NaCl(l) → 2Na + Cl₂ (Downs cell)
Rusting
4Fe + 3O₂ + xH₂O → 2Fe₂O₃·xH₂O  |  2Ag + H₂S → Ag₂S (black) + H₂
📋 Top Board Tips
💡 Reactivity Series: Memorise the Order
Mnemonic: Please Stop Calling Me A Zany Iron Person Can Save Gold Platinum (K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al, Zn, Fe, Pb, Cu, Ag, Au, Pt). Position determines extraction method + displacement ability.
💡 Rust Formula: Always Write ·xH₂O
The correct formula is Fe₂O₃·xH₂O — not just Fe₂O₃. Missing the ·xH₂O will cost you marks every time. Also: both O₂ AND H₂O are needed for rusting — neither alone causes rust.
💡 Ionic Conduction: The Key Rule
Ionic solid → does NOT conduct (ions fixed in lattice). Ionic molten/solution → DOES conduct (ions free to move). Sugar solution = no conduction (no ions formed). This distinction appears in MCQs and case-based questions every year.
💡 Galvanised vs Tin-Plated
Zn > Fe: galvanised iron has sacrificial protection even after scratching. Sn < Fe: tin-plated iron corrodes FASTER when scratched (Fe becomes anode). This comparison is asked almost every 2 years.
💡 Blast Furnace: CO is the Reducing Agent
Many students write "carbon reduces iron ore" — but the actual reducing agent is CO, not C directly. C burns to CO₂, then CO₂ + C → 2CO, and it's CO that reduces Fe₂O₃. This is the most common MCQ trap in this chapter.
💡 Alloys: Name, Composition, Use
Always give all three: name + composition + use. Brass (Cu+Zn) ≠ Bronze (Cu+Sn) — most confused pair. Solder (Pb+Sn) has LOWER m.p. than either pure metal (eutectic mixture). German silver has NO silver (Cu+Zn+Ni).
