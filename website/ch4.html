Chapter Overview

Chapter 4 is one of the largest and most consistently tested chapters in Class 10 boards — carrying 8–12 marks every year. It introduces the unique chemistry of carbon: catenation, tetravalency, allotropes, homologous series, functional groups, naming conventions, and all major reactions of organic compounds. Ethanol and ethanoic acid properties, esterification, saponification, and the cleansing action of soaps are guaranteed board topics.

⚛️
Bonding in Carbon
Carbon always forms covalent bonds (sharing electrons). Tetravalency = 4 bonds with C, H, O, N, halogens. Catenation = C–C bond formation (chains, branches, rings). Both properties together = millions of organic compounds.
💎
Allotropes of Carbon
Diamond: 4 bonds per C, 3D tetrahedral, hardest, insulator. Graphite: 3 bonds per C, layered hexagonal, conductor, soft lubricant. Fullerene (C₆₀): spherical cage, 20 hexagons + 12 pentagons, nanotechnology applications.
📊
Homologous Series
Same general formula, same functional group, differ by –CH₂– (14u), similar chemical properties. Series: Alkanes CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ · Alkenes CₙH₂ₙ · Alkynes CₙH₂ₙ₋₂ · Alcohols CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH · Carboxylic acids CₙH₂ₙ₊₁COOH.
🔗
Functional Groups
–OH (alcohol) · –COOH (carboxylic acid) · –CHO (aldehyde) · –CO– (ketone) · C=C (alkene) · –Cl/–Br (haloalkane). The functional group determines the chemical behaviour of the compound.
🔥
Chemical Reactions
Combustion (CO₂ + H₂O) · Addition (unsaturated, e.g. Br₂ decolourises) · Substitution (saturated + Cl₂ in sunlight) · Oxidation (ethanol → ethanoic acid via KMnO₄/K₂Cr₂O₇) · Esterification (acid + alcohol ⇌ ester + water).
🍶
Ethanol
C₂H₅OH. Made by fermentation (C₆H₁₂O₆ →(yeast)→ 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂). Reacts with Na (H₂↑), dehydrates at 170°C (→ ethene) or 140°C (→ ether), oxidises to CH₃COOH. Used in beverages, fuel, solvent. Methanol (CH₃OH) is toxic.
🍋
Ethanoic Acid
CH₃COOH. Glacial = pure, freezes at 16.6°C. Vinegar = 5% solution. Reacts with NaHCO₃ (CO₂↑), NaOH, Na₂CO₃, and undergoes esterification. Turns litmus red. The –COOH group test = CO₂ with NaHCO₃.
🧼
Soaps & Detergents
Soap: saponification of fat with NaOH (hard) or KOH (soft). Structure: hydrophobic tail + hydrophilic head. Micelle formation cleans grease. Soaps fail in hard water (scum). Detergents work in hard water but some are non-biodegradable.
⚗️ Must-Know Equations
Fermentation
C₆H₁₂O₆ →(yeast, 25-30°C, anaerobic)→ 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂
Esterification
CH₃COOH + C₂H₅OH ⇌ CH₃COOC₂H₅ + H₂O  (conc. H₂SO₄, heat)
Saponification
Fat + 3NaOH → 3 Soap (sodium fatty acid salt) + Glycerol
Dehydration
C₂H₅OH →(excess conc. H₂SO₄, 170°C)→ CH₂=CH₂ + H₂O
Oxidation
C₂H₅OH →(alk. KMnO₄ or K₂Cr₂O₇)→ CH₃COOH (ethanoic acid)
Addition
CH₂=CH₂ + H₂ →(Ni, 150°C)→ CH₃–CH₃  |  CH₂=CH₂ + Br₂ → CH₂BrCH₂Br
Substitution

NaHCO₃ test
CH₃COOH + NaHCO₃ → CH₃COONa + H₂O + CO₂↑ (brisk effervescence)
📋 Top Board Tips
💡 Sat. vs Unsat.: The Golden Rule
Saturated → Substitution. Alkanes (all single bonds) undergo substitution reactions (e.g. CH₄ + Cl₂ in sunlight). Unsaturated → Addition. Alkenes/Alkynes (have π bond) undergo addition (e.g. + H₂, + Br₂). This distinction is tested as MCQ every 2–3 years.
💡 Esterification: Two Common Errors
Error 1: Writing → instead of ⇌. Esterification IS reversible — always use ⇌. Error 2: Writing only "catalyst" for H₂SO₄. It is BOTH a catalyst AND a dehydrating agent — state both for full marks. These two errors together lose 2 marks every time.
💡 Test for –COOH vs –OH
Ethanoic acid + NaHCO₃ → CO₂↑ (brisk effervescence, turns lime water milky). Ethanol + NaHCO₃ → no reaction. This is the test that distinguishes carboxylic acids from alcohols. Litmus test also works: acid turns blue → red; alcohol = no change.
💡 Diamond vs Graphite: Always Explain Structurally
Never say "graphite is soft" without saying WHY. Correct: "Graphite has layered structure; layers held by weak forces → slide easily → soft." Same for conductivity: "Graphite has delocalised electrons between layers → conducts electricity." Structure → property chains score full marks.
💡 Dehydration Temperature Matters
C₂H₅OH + excess conc. H₂SO₄ at 170°C → Ethene. Same reaction at 140°C → Diethyl ether. The temperature determines the product. This condition-product distinction is directly asked in boards as both MCQ and 2-mark questions.
💡 Count the –COOH Carbon in Naming
When naming carboxylic acids, always count the –COOH carbon as C1. CH₃COOH = 2C = ethanoic acid (not methanoic). Many students name only the alkyl part and forget to count the carbonyl carbon. This error costs 1 mark in every naming question.
