MCQPYQ 2020
Which of the following is a strong acid?
A. Acetic acid (CH₃COOH)
B. Carbonic acid (H₂CO₃)
C. Hydrochloric acid (HCl)
D. Formic acid (HCOOH)
▼
✅ Answer: C
HCl is a strong acid — it completely ionises in water to give H⁺ ions. Acetic, formic, and carbonic acids are weak acids that only partially ionise.
Board Tip: Strong acids → HCl, H₂SO₄, HNO₃. Weak acids → CH₃COOH, H₂CO₃, H₃PO₄.

MCQPYQ 2019
The pH of a solution is 3. What is the nature of the solution?
A. Strongly acidic
B. Weakly acidic
C. Neutral
D. Basic
▼
✅ Answer: A
pH scale: 0–6 = acidic, 7 = neutral, 8–14 = basic. pH 3 is strongly acidic (lower pH = stronger acid). pH 5–6 would be weakly acidic.
Board Tip: pH < 7 → acidic. pH = 7 → neutral. pH > 7 → basic/alkaline. Always state this in 1-mark answers.

MCQPYQ 2017
What happens to the pH of an acid solution when it is diluted with water?
A. Decreases
B. Increases
C. Remains the same
D. First decreases then increases
▼
✅ Answer: B
Diluting an acid reduces H⁺ ion concentration, so pH increases (moves toward 7 but never exceeds 7 for an acid). Diluting a base decreases pH (moves toward 7).
Board Tip: Dilution always moves pH toward 7. Acid → pH increases. Base → pH decreases.

Why do acids not show acidic behaviour in the absence of water?
▼
✅ Answer

SA I · 2MPYQ 2023
Write the chemical equation for the reaction of dilute HCl with (i) zinc, and (ii) sodium hydroxide.
▼
✅ Answer
(i) Acid + Metal → Salt + Hydrogen gas:
Zn(s) + 2HCl(aq) → ZnCl₂(aq) + H₂(g)↑
(ii) Acid + Base (Neutralisation) → Salt + Water:
HCl(aq) + NaOH(aq) → NaCl(aq) + H₂O(l)
Board Tip: Always include state symbols (s, l, g, aq) and ↑/↓ where applicable — each missing state symbol may cost marks.

SA I · 2MPYQ 2017
Explain why: (i) Concentrated H₂SO₄ should be added to water and not water to acid. (ii) Acid rain damages marble statues.
▼
✅ Answer
(i) Diluting concentrated H₂SO₄ is highly exothermic. If water is added to acid, the intense heat causes the water to boil suddenly, splashing the concentrated acid dangerously. Adding acid to water gradually, while stirring, distributes heat safely.

(ii) Marble = CaCO₃. Acid rain contains H₂SO₄ and HNO₃ (pH < 5.6). These react with marble:
CaCO₃ + H₂SO₄ → CaSO₄ + H₂O + CO₂↑
This dissolves the marble surface, damaging statues and buildings.
Board Tip: Part (ii) uses the acid + carbonate reaction. Acid rain pH < 5.6 — this pH value is frequently asked in MCQ too.

This is a neutralisation reaction (acid + base → salt + water). It is also exothermic — heat is released. Products: sodium chloride (NaCl = common salt) and water.

SA I · 2MPYQ 2015
How do metallic oxides differ from non-metallic oxides in terms of their acidic/basic nature? Give one example of each.
▼
✅ Answer
Metallic oxides are basic in nature. They react with acids to form salt and water. Example: CaO (calcium oxide) + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ (base).

Non-metallic oxides are acidic in nature. They react with water to form acids, and with bases to form salt and water. Example: CO₂ + H₂O → H₂CO₃ (carbonic acid).
Board Tip: This distinction is a key NCERT concept. Note that some metal oxides (like ZnO, Al₂O₃) are amphoteric — they act as both acid and base.

SA I · 2MPYQ 2023
Why is tooth decay related to pH? How can tooth decay be prevented?
▼
✅ Answer
Bacteria in the mouth break down food particles and produce acids. When pH in the mouth falls below 5.5, the acids start corroding the enamel (calcium phosphate) of teeth, leading to tooth decay.

Prevention: (i) Use toothpastes containing mild bases (fluorides) — they neutralise the acid. (ii) Rinse mouth after eating. (iii) Avoid sugary foods that feed acid-producing bacteria.
Board Tip: Critical pH for tooth decay = 5.5. Always mention this specific value in tooth decay answers.

SA I · 2MPYQ 2022
On what basis are salts classified as acidic, basic, and neutral? Give one example of each.
▼
✅ Answer
Salts are classified based on the strength of the acid and base that formed them:
• Neutral salt: Formed by strong acid + strong base. pH ≈ 7. Example: NaCl (HCl + NaOH).
• Acidic salt: Formed by strong acid + weak base. pH < 7. Example: NH₄Cl (HCl + NH₄OH).
• Basic salt: Formed by weak acid + strong base. pH > 7. Example: Na₂CO₃ (H₂CO₃ + NaOH).

LA · 5MPYQ 2020
(a) What is meant by the strength of an acid/base? How is it different from concentration? (b) Draw and explain the pH scale. (c) Give 4 examples of how pH affects living organisms and daily life.
▼
✅ Answer
(a) Strength: A strong acid/base completely ionises in water (e.g., HCl, NaOH). A weak acid/base partially ionises (e.g., CH₃COOH, NH₃). Strength depends on the degree of ionisation.
Concentration: Amount of acid/base dissolved per unit volume of solution. A concentrated weak acid is still a weak acid. Don't confuse "concentrated" with "strong" — they are different properties.

(b) pH Scale: Runs from 0–14. pH measures H⁺ ion concentration. pH = –log[H⁺].
• pH 0–6 → Acidic (lower = more acidic)
• pH 7 → Neutral (pure water)
• pH 8–14 → Basic (higher = more basic)
Universal indicator gives different colours: red (0–2), orange (3–4), yellow (5–6), green (7), blue (8–10), violet (11–14).

(c) pH in daily life:
1. Digestion: Stomach pH ≈ 2 for HCl digestion; antacids raise pH.
2. Tooth decay: pH below 5.5 corrodes enamel.
3. Agriculture: Soil pH 6–7 for most crops; lime added to acidic soil.
4. Aquatic organisms: Fish survive only at pH 7–8; acid rain destroys aquatic life.

LA · 5MPYQ 2015
(a) What are salts? How are they formed? (b) Give one example each of acidic, basic, and neutral salt. (c) Explain why the pH of NaCl solution is 7 while Na₂CO₃ has pH > 7. (d) What is common salt and how is it obtained?
▼
✅ Answer
(a) Salts: Ionic compounds formed by the neutralisation of an acid and a base. The cation comes from the base; anion from the acid. General: Acid + Base → Salt + Water.

(b) Examples:
• Acidic salt (strong acid + weak base): NH₄Cl — NH₃ is weak base, HCl is strong acid. pH < 7.
• Basic salt (weak acid + strong base): Na₂CO₃ — H₂CO₃ is weak acid, NaOH is strong base. pH > 7.
• Neutral salt (strong acid + strong base): NaCl — HCl + NaOH. pH = 7.

(c) NaCl (strong acid HCl + strong base NaOH) → both completely ionise, no hydrolysis, pure neutral. pH = 7. Na₂CO₃ (weak acid H₂CO₃ + strong base NaOH) → CO₃²⁻ ion hydrolyses in water: CO₃²⁻ + H₂O → HCO₃⁻ + OH⁻. Excess OH⁻ ions make solution alkaline. pH > 7.

(d) Common salt = NaCl. Obtained: (i) By evaporation of sea water (solar evaporation in salt pans). (ii) By mining rock salt deposits. Used in food, making other sodium compounds (baking soda, washing soda, NaOH).
