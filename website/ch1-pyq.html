MCQPYQ 2020
Identify 'x', 'y' and 'z' in the balanced equation: 2KClO₃ →(x) 2KCl + 3O₂ where x is above the arrow, (s) or (g) labels are missing.
A. x = gas; y = reaction condition; z = gas
B. x = physical state of KClO₃ and KCl; y = reaction condition; z = physical state of O₂
C. x = number of moles of KClO₃; y = reaction condition; z = number of molecules of O₂
D. x = solid; y = liquid; z = gas
▼
✅ Answer: B
In a chemical equation, the letters in brackets (s), (l), (g), (aq) represent the physical states of substances. 'x' represents the physical states (s) for KClO₃ and KCl; 'y' represents the reaction condition (heat/MnO₂); 'z' represents the physical state (g) for O₂.

MCQPYQ 2021
Assertion (A): Burning of natural gas is an endothermic process.
Reason (R): Methane gas combines with oxygen to produce carbon dioxide and water.
A. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
B. Both A and R are true, but R is NOT the correct explanation of A
C. A is true but R is false
D. A is false but R is true
▼
✅ Answer: D
A is false — burning of natural gas (combustion of methane) is actually an exothermic process, not endothermic. It releases heat and light. R is true — methane does combine with oxygen to produce CO₂ and water. Since A is false but R is true, the answer is D.
Board Tip: Combustion = always exothermic. Assertion-Reason: check A first, then R, then whether R explains A.

MCQPYQ 2023
When aqueous solutions of potassium iodide and lead nitrate are mixed, an insoluble substance separates out. The correct balanced chemical equation for the reaction is:
A. KI + PbNO₃ → PbI + KNO₃
B. 2KI + Pb(NO₃)₂ → PbI₂↓ + 2KNO₃
C. KI + Pb(NO₃)₂ → PbI + KNO₃
D. KI + PbNO₃ → PbI₂ + KNO₃
▼
✅ Answer: B
2KI(aq) + Pb(NO₃)₂(aq) → PbI₂(s)↓ + 2KNO₃(aq). This is a double displacement (precipitation) reaction. The yellow precipitate is lead iodide (PbI₂). The equation must be balanced — 2 KI needed to provide 2 I⁻ ions for one Pb²⁺ ion.
Board Tip: Always check balancing. Lead iodide is yellow — a commonly tested observation.

When lead nitrate powder is heated in a boiling tube, we observe:

C. Yellow fumes of nitrogen dioxide

MCQPYQ 2021
The endothermic process(es) among the following is/are:
I. Dilution of H₂SO₄   II. Sublimation of dry ice   III. Condensation of water vapour   IV. Dissolution of NH₄Cl in water
A. I and III
B. II only
C. III only
D. II and IV
▼
✅ Answer: D
Endothermic = absorbs heat from surroundings. Sublimation of dry ice absorbs heat (solid CO₂ → gas). Dissolution of NH₄Cl is endothermic (flask feels cold). Dilution of H₂SO₄ is exothermic (gives heat). Condensation of water vapour is exothermic.
Board Tip: Sublimation and dissolution of ammonium salts = endothermic. Condensation and dilution of acids = exothermic.

MCQPYQ 2023
A solution 'X' turns milky when CO₂ is passed through it. After some time, it becomes clear. On passing more CO₂ it turns milky again. Solution X is:
A. NaOH solution
B. Ca(OH)₂ solution (lime water)
C. Na₂CO₃ solution
D. KOH solution
▼
✅ Answer: B

MCQPYQ 2022
In the reaction: CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O, which substance is being oxidised and which is being reduced?
A. CuO is oxidised; H₂ is reduced
B. H₂ is oxidised; CuO is reduced
C. Both CuO and H₂ are oxidised
D. Neither is oxidised or reduced

MCQ
Electrolysis of water is a decomposition reaction. The ratio of volumes of H₂ : O₂ collected at cathode and anode respectively is:
A. 1:2
B. 2:1
C. 1:1
D. 4:1
▼
✅ Answer: B
2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂. 2 moles of H₂ are produced for every 1 mole of O₂. Since volume is proportional to moles (at same T and P), H₂:O₂ = 2:1. H₂ is collected at cathode, O₂ at anode.

Explain with an example: Can a displacement reaction also be a redox reaction?
▼
✅ Answer
Yes! Many displacement reactions are also redox reactions. Example:

▼
✅ Answer
Oxidation: Gain of oxygen OR loss of hydrogen by a substance.
Reduction: Loss of oxygen OR gain of hydrogen by a substance.
Both occur simultaneously in a redox reaction.
Oxidising agent: Causes oxidation in another; itself gets reduced.
Reducing agent: Causes reduction in another; itself gets oxidised.

CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O
H₂ is reducing agent (oxidised to H₂O); CuO is oxidising agent (reduced to Cu).

The teacher explained that these are both very important types of reactions and that the yellow precipitate is actually a beautiful demonstration called "Golden Rain."
(i) Name the brown gas evolved in the first activity and write its chemical formula. (1)
(ii) Write the balanced equation for the first activity. Name the type of reaction. (1)
(iii) Write the balanced equation for the second activity ("Golden Rain"). Name the precipitate. (1)
(iv) Name the type of reaction in the second activity. Why is a precipitate formed? (1)
▼
✅ Answers
(i) Nitrogen dioxide (NO₂) — brown/reddish-brown gas.
(ii)
2Pb(NO₃)₂(s) →(heat) 2PbO(s) + 4NO₂(g)↑ + O₂(g)↑
Type: Thermal decomposition reaction.
(iii)
2KI(aq) + Pb(NO₃)₂(aq) → PbI₂(s)↓ + 2KNO₃(aq)
Precipitate: Lead iodide (PbI₂), bright yellow.
(iv) Double displacement (precipitation) reaction. A precipitate forms because PbI₂ is insoluble in water — the product ions cannot remain dissolved and settle out as a solid.

Passage: Ananya's mother was concerned that the ghee kept in the kitchen had started smelling unpleasant and tasted bitter. When Ananya told her chemistry teacher about this, the teacher explained that this is a chemical process called rancidity. The teacher also explained that the same problem affects ships and automobiles, but in a different way. The teacher told Ananya three ways to prevent the food problem.
(i) What is rancidity? What causes the unpleasant smell and taste? (1)
(ii) Name the similar problem affecting metals and give its chemical name. Write the equation for rusting of iron. (1)
(iii) State two methods the teacher might have suggested to prevent rancidity. (1)
(iv) State one method to prevent corrosion of iron (not painting) and explain how it works. (1)
▼
✅ Answers
(i) Rancidity is the slow oxidation of fats and oils by atmospheric oxygen, producing compounds (peroxides, aldehydes) with unpleasant smell and taste.
(ii) Corrosion. Rust = hydrated ferric oxide (Fe₂O₃·xH₂O).
4Fe + 3O₂ + xH₂O → 2Fe₂O₃·xH₂O
(iii) Adding antioxidants (BHA) and storing in airtight containers / flushing with nitrogen gas.
(iv) Galvanisation — coating iron with zinc. Zinc is more reactive than iron and gets preferentially oxidised, protecting iron underneath (sacrificial protection). Even if scratched, the zinc continues to protect the iron.

Passage: During a chemistry class, the teacher placed a piece of magnesium ribbon in a Bunsen burner flame. A dazzling white light was produced. Later, the teacher dissolved the white powder formed in water and tested it with litmus. The solution turned red litmus blue. In another demonstration, white solid X was added to water and the mixture became very hot. The resulting solution also turned red litmus blue.
(i) Write the balanced equation for burning of magnesium ribbon. Name the type of reaction. (1)
(ii) What does the litmus test indicate about the product of magnesium burning? (1)
(iii) Identify substance X. Write the balanced equation for X dissolving in water. Name the type of reaction. (1)
(iv) Both products turned red litmus blue. What does this indicate? What is the common name of the product of X + water reaction? (1)
▼
✅ Answers
(i)
2Mg(s) + O₂(g) → 2MgO(s)
Combination reaction (exothermic).
(ii) MgO dissolved in water forms Mg(OH)₂ which is a base (alkaline). The blue litmus indicates the solution is basic/alkaline (pH > 7).
(iii) X = CaO (quicklime/calcium oxide).
CaO(s) + H₂O(l) → Ca(OH)₂(aq) + Heat
Combination reaction (exothermic — mixture becomes very hot).
(iv) Both turn red litmus blue → both products are basic/alkaline. Common name of Ca(OH)₂: Slaked lime.

Passage: A chemistry teacher explained to Class 10 students that in ancient Egypt, gold and silver artefacts survived thousands of years in tombs with almost no degradation, while iron tools found in the same tombs had completely crumbled away. She further explained that while iron corrodes rapidly, gold and silver are much less reactive. She also mentioned that silver articles do tarnish over time due to a specific reaction with the atmosphere, and that copper slowly turns green.
(i) What name is given to the slow deterioration of metals by atmospheric reaction? Name the product formed on iron and give its formula. (1)
(ii) Why did gold artefacts survive but not iron? What chemical property explains this? (1)
(iii) Write the reaction responsible for tarnishing of silver. Name the compound formed. (1)
(iv) Copper turns green due to reaction with CO₂ and moisture forming basic copper carbonate. Write the probable chemical formula and name the type of reaction. (1)
▼
✅ Answers
(i) Corrosion. Product on iron: Rust = Hydrated ferric oxide (Fe₂O₃·xH₂O).
(ii) Gold is at the bottom of the reactivity series — it is the least reactive metal and does not react with atmospheric oxygen, moisture, or CO₂. Iron is highly reactive in comparison.
(iii)
4Ag + 2H₂S + O₂ → 2Ag₂S + 2H₂O
Compound: Silver sulphide (Ag₂S) — black in colour.
(iv) Basic copper carbonate: Cu(OH)₂·CuCO₃ (also written as Cu₂(OH)₂CO₃). Type: Combination/Oxidation reaction (copper gains oxygen/reacts with atmospheric CO₂ and moisture).

Passage: Rohan performed an experiment: he poured dilute H₂SO₄ onto zinc granules in a test tube and collected the gas over water. He brought a burning splint near the collected gas and heard a pop sound. He also noticed the test tube became warm. He then tried the same with copper granules and observed no reaction. His teacher explained this is because of the reactivity series.
(i) Name the gas collected and identify the type of reaction. Write the balanced equation. (1)
(ii) Why did the test tube become warm? What type of reaction is this (exo/endothermic)? (1)
(iii) Why did copper not react with dilute H₂SO₄? (1)
(iv) If Rohan tried silver instead of zinc, would a reaction occur? Give a reason using the reactivity series. (1)
▼
✅ Answers
(i) Hydrogen gas (H₂) — burns with a pop sound. Displacement reaction.
Zn(s) + H₂SO₄(aq) → ZnSO₄(aq) + H₂(g)↑
(ii) The test tube became warm because the reaction is exothermic — heat is released. Exothermic reaction.
(iii) Copper is below hydrogen in the reactivity series (Zn > Fe > H > Cu > Ag). Copper is less reactive than hydrogen, so it cannot displace hydrogen from an acid. No reaction occurs.
(iv) No, silver would not react. Silver is even less reactive than copper, placed below it in the reactivity series. Only metals above hydrogen in the reactivity series can displace H₂ from dilute acids.
