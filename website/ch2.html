Chapter Overview

Chapter 2 is one of the highest-scoring chapters in Class 10 boards — carrying consistent 7–10 marks every year. It covers acids and bases from first principles: pH, indicators, reaction types — right through to the industrial importance of salts like NaOH, Na₂CO₃, NaHCO₃, and POP. Memorise definitions, equations, and uses of all important salts to score full marks.

🔴
Acids
Sour taste · blue litmus → red · produce H⁺ in water. Strong: HCl, H₂SO₄, HNO₃. Weak: CH₃COOH, H₂CO₃. Dry HCl ≠ acidic (no water = no H⁺).
🔵
Bases & Alkalis
Bitter · soapy · red litmus → blue · produce OH⁻ in water. Alkali = water-soluble base. Strong: NaOH, KOH. Amphoteric: ZnO, Al₂O₃ (react with both acid and base).
🌈
Indicators & pH
Litmus · phenolphthalein · methyl orange · turmeric · universal indicator. pH 0–14. pH < 7 = acid · 7 = neutral · > 7 = base. Tooth decay: pH below 5.5.
⚗️
Reactions of Acids & Bases
5 types: with metals → H₂; metal oxides → salt+water; carbonates → CO₂; bicarbonates → CO₂; bases → neutralisation. All 5 need equations + observations.
🧂
Important Salts
NaCl · NaOH (caustic soda) · Na₂CO₃·10H₂O (washing soda) · NaHCO₃ (baking soda) · CaOCl₂ (bleaching powder) · CaSO₄·½H₂O (Plaster of Paris).
🏭
Industrial Processes
Chlor-alkali: brine electrolysis → NaOH + Cl₂ + H₂. POP from gypsum at 100°C. Bleaching powder: Ca(OH)₂ + Cl₂. Baking soda via Solvay process.
⚗️ Must-Know Equations
Acid + Metal
Zn + H₂SO₄ → ZnSO₄ + H₂↑  |  Fe + 2HCl → FeCl₂ + H₂↑
Neutralisation
NaOH + HCl → NaCl + H₂O  |  Acid + Base → Salt + Water + Heat
Acid + Carbonate
Na₂CO₃ + 2HCl → 2NaCl + H₂O + CO₂↑  |  NaHCO₃ + HCl → NaCl + H₂O + CO₂↑
Chlor-Alkali
2NaCl(aq) + 2H₂O(l) →(electrolysis)→ 2NaOH + Cl₂↑ + H₂↑
POP & Baking
2NaHCO₃ →(heat)→ Na₂CO₃ + H₂O + CO₂↑  |  CaSO₄·2H₂O →(100°C)→ CaSO₄·½H₂O + 1½H₂O
Amphoteric Zn
Zn + 2NaOH → Na₂ZnO₂ + H₂↑  |  2Al + 2NaOH + 2H₂O → 2NaAlO₂ + 3H₂↑
📋 Top Board Tips
💡 Dry HCl & Litmus
Dry HCl does NOT change dry litmus. Acids show acidic properties ONLY in water — H⁺ ions form only in aqueous solution. Guaranteed board question every year.
💡 Salt Classification
SA + SB = Neutral (NaCl, pH 7). WA + SB = Basic (Na₂CO₃, pH > 7). SA + WB = Acidic (NH₄Cl, pH < 7). Learn this rule for instant 1-mark answers.
💡 Dilution Precaution
Always add acid to water — never water to acid. Diluting H₂SO₄ is exothermic. Adding water to acid causes violent boiling and dangerous splashing.
💡 Water of Crystallisation
Gypsum = 2H₂O · POP = ½H₂O · Washing soda = 10H₂O · Blue vitriol = 5H₂O. White anhydrous CuSO₄ turns blue on adding water — this is the test for water.
💡 Baking Powder vs Soda
Baking soda alone → Na₂CO₃ (bitter, soapy). Baking powder = NaHCO₃ + tartaric acid. Tartaric acid neutralises Na₂CO₃ → no bitter taste, fluffy cake.
💡 Chlor-Alkali Products
Anode → Cl₂. Cathode → H₂. Solution → NaOH. All 3 products with 2 uses each are commonly asked in 3-mark and 5-mark questions — memorise all 6 uses.
