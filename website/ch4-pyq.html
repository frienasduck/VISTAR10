MCQPYQ 2019
The general formula for alkanes is:
A. CₙH₂ₙ₊₂
B. CₙH₂ₙ
C. CₙH₂ₙ₋₂
D. CₙHₙ
▼
✅ Answer: A — CₙH₂ₙ₊₂
Alkanes are saturated hydrocarbons (only C–C single bonds). General formula CₙH₂ₙ₊₂. Methane CH₄ (n=1), Ethane C₂H₆ (n=2), Propane C₃H₈ (n=3).
Board Tip: Three formulas: Alkane CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ · Alkene CₙH₂ₙ · Alkyne CₙH₂ₙ₋₂. Each series loses 2H due to the extra bond. Alcohols: CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH. Carboxylic acids: CₙH₂ₙ₊₁COOH.

MCQPYQ 2021
Ethanol reacts with sodium metal to produce:
A. CO₂
B. H₂
C. O₂
D. CH₄
▼
✅ Answer: B — H₂
2C₂H₅OH + 2Na → 2C₂H₅ONa + H₂↑. The –OH group reacts with Na, releasing hydrogen. "Pop" sound with burning splint confirms H₂.
Board Tip: Ethanol + Na → H₂↑ + sodium ethoxide. Less vigorous than water + Na because ethanol is a much weaker acid. Pop sound = test for H₂.

MCQPYQ 2022
Which of the following decolourises bromine water?
A. Methane
B. Ethane
C. Ethene
D. Propane
▼
✅ Answer: C — Ethene
Ethene (CH₂=CH₂) has a C=C double bond. It undergoes addition with Br₂: CH₂=CH₂ + Br₂ → CH₂BrCH₂Br, turning orange Br₂ water colourless. Alkanes (methane, ethane, propane) are saturated — no reaction with Br₂ water.
Board Tip: Decolourisation of bromine water = test for unsaturation (C=C or C≡C). Saturated hydrocarbons do NOT decolourise. This is the key practical test — appears in boards almost every year.

MCQPYQ 2020
Saponification is the process of:
A. Making esters from acids and alcohols
B. Burning fats in oxygen
C. Alkaline hydrolysis of fats to give soap and glycerol
D. Hydrogenation of vegetable oils
▼
✅ Answer: C
Fat + NaOH → soap (sodium salt of fatty acid) + glycerol. NaOH → hard soap. KOH → soft/liquid soap. This alkaline hydrolysis of fat is saponification.
Board Tip: Saponification ≠ esterification. Esterification: acid + alcohol → ester (H₂SO₄, reversible). Saponification: fat + NaOH → soap + glycerol (not reversible, base as reagent not catalyst).

MCQPYQ 2017
In CH₄ + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + HCl, the type of reaction is:
A. Addition
B. Substitution
C. Combustion
D. Esterification
▼
✅ Answer: B — Substitution
One H atom in methane is replaced (substituted) by Cl. Requires sunlight (hν). Saturated hydrocarbons (alkanes) undergo substitution — they cannot undergo addition as they have no double/triple bonds.
Board Tip: Saturated → Substitution. Unsaturated → Addition. Sunlight is ESSENTIAL for CH₄ + Cl₂. In darkness the reaction does not occur. This distinction is directly tested as MCQ every 2–3 years.

MCQPYQ 2023
Which of the following is NOT an allotrope of carbon?
A. Diamond
B. Graphite
C. Buckminsterfullerene
D. Coal
▼
✅ Answer: D — Coal
Diamond, Graphite, and Buckminsterfullerene (C₆₀) are crystalline allotropes of carbon. Coal is amorphous (non-crystalline) carbon — a complex mixture, not a pure allotrope. Charcoal, coke, and soot are also amorphous forms.
Board Tip: Three allotropes: Diamond (3D tetrahedral), Graphite (layered hexagonal), Fullerene (spherical C₆₀). Coal/charcoal/coke = amorphous carbon = NOT allotropes. All three allotrope structures and their properties are tested.

▼

MCQPYQ 2016
Consecutive members of a homologous series differ by:
A. –CH₃ (15 mass units)
B. –CH₂– (14 mass units)
C. –OH (17 mass units)
D. –CO– (28 mass units)
▼
✅ Answer: B — –CH₂– (14 mass units)
In any homologous series, consecutive members differ by one –CH₂– unit = 14 u (C=12, 2H=2). For alcohols: Methanol (32) → Ethanol (46) → Propanol (60), each +14. This is a defining characteristic of all homologous series.
Board Tip: 14 = mass of –CH₂– unit. If two molecular masses in a series differ by a multiple of 14, they could belong to the same series. Always calculate: C(12) + 2×H(1) = 14.

VSAPYQ 2017
What type of bond does carbon always form?
▼
✅ Answer: Covalent bonds
Carbon always forms covalent bonds (electron sharing). It neither easily loses 4 electrons (too much energy) nor gains 4 electrons (too much repulsion). So it shares electrons, forming up to 4 covalent bonds simultaneously.

Define a homologous series.
▼

▼
✅ Answer

SA IPYQ 2016
Why are carbon compounds generally poor conductors of electricity?
▼
✅ Answer
Carbon compounds are covalent — they share electrons rather than forming ions. They have no free ions or free electrons to carry electric current. Therefore they are poor conductors. Exception: graphite has delocalised electrons and conducts electricity.

LAPYQ 2015
Explain in detail: Why does carbon form covalent bonds? Explain catenation and tetravalency with examples. How do these two properties together explain the diversity of carbon compounds?
▼
✅ Answer
Why covalent bonds: Carbon has 4 valence electrons. To become stable, it needs 4 more electrons. But losing 4 e⁻ requires too much energy (high ionisation energy), and gaining 4 e⁻ causes too much repulsion. So carbon shares 4 electrons → forms 4 covalent bonds.

Catenation: Carbon atoms form stable bonds with each other (C–C, C=C, C≡C). This allows formation of: straight chains (n-butane), branched chains (isobutane), and rings (cyclopropane, benzene). The C–C bond is strong enough (~347 kJ/mol) to form long stable chains — unique to carbon.

Tetravalency: Carbon forms 4 bonds simultaneously with: other C atoms, H, O, N, Cl, Br, S. It can form single, double, and triple bonds. This allows attachment of different atoms/groups to the same carbon backbone.

Together: Catenation creates the backbone (chain/ring of any length). Tetravalency fills the remaining bonds with different atoms/functional groups. Together, they generate virtually unlimited structural possibilities — the source of millions of organic compounds.

Board Tip: Four parts = 4 marks. Each identification (A=ethanol, B=ethanoic acid, C=ethyl ethanoate) must be justified using the clues given in the passage. Simply naming them without justification loses marks in case-based questions.

Case BasedPYQ 2017
Read the following and answer the questions:
A student had a compound X with molecular formula C₂H₄. She added it to orange bromine water, and the orange colour disappeared completely. She then added X to H₂ gas in the presence of a Ni catalyst. Finally, she added the product to orange bromine water — this time the colour did not disappear.
(i) Identify compound X. To which homologous series does it belong?
(ii) What happened when X was added to bromine water? Write the equation.
(iii) What is the product when X reacts with H₂? Is this product saturated or unsaturated?
(iv) Explain why the product did not decolourise bromine water.
▼
✅ Answer
(i) X = Ethene (CH₂=CH₂). Molecular formula C₂H₄ = CₙH₂ₙ with n=2. Belongs to Alkenes homologous series (general formula CₙH₂ₙ).

(ii) X + Bromine water: Addition reaction. CH₂=CH₂ + Br₂ → CH₂Br–CH₂Br (1,2-dibromoethane, colourless). Orange Br₂ → colourless = decolourisation confirms C=C bond.

(iii) X + H₂: CH₂=CH₂ + H₂ →(Ni, 150°C)→ CH₃–CH₃ (ethane). Product is saturated (only C–C single bonds).

(iv) Ethane + Br₂ water: Ethane is a saturated alkane — it has no C=C double bond for Br₂ to add across. Saturated compounds do not decolourise bromine water (they can undergo substitution with Br₂ in sunlight, but not addition with Br₂ in water).
Board Tip: This case demonstrates: unsaturated → addition → decolourises Br₂. After hydrogenation → saturated → no addition → Br₂ not decolourised. The before/after Br₂ test comparison is a perfect board experiment design.

Case BasedPYQ 2016
Read the following and answer the questions:
In a town, a batch of illicit liquor (hooch) caused several people to go blind and some died. The police found that the liquor contained methanol instead of ethanol. A doctor explained to the media that methanol is very dangerous for human consumption, even in small quantities. A student who heard this news wondered why methanol and ethanol, which look and smell so similar, have such different effects.
(i) Give the molecular formulae of methanol and ethanol.
(ii) Why is methanol so dangerous? What happens inside the body when methanol is consumed?
(iii) How would you distinguish between methanol and ethanol in a chemistry laboratory?
(iv) What is the common source of ethanol for beverages?
▼
✅ Answer
(i) Methanol: CH₃OH. Ethanol: C₂H₅OH. Both have –OH group but differ in carbon chain length (1C vs 2C).

(ii) Methanol toxicity: In the body, liver enzymes oxidise methanol to formaldehyde (HCHO) and then to formic acid — both highly toxic. Formaldehyde damages the optic nerve → permanent blindness (as little as 5–10 mL). Higher doses (30+ mL) can be fatal. There is no antidote for methanol poisoning in its advanced stages.

(iii) Distinguishing methanol and ethanol: Simple chemical tests cannot easily distinguish them. Best method is distillation and boiling point measurement: methanol b.p. = 64.7°C; ethanol b.p. = 78.4°C. Advanced: chromatography or oxidation to distinguish formaldehyde (from CH₃OH) vs acetaldehyde/acetic acid (from C₂H₅OH).

(iv) Ethanol for beverages: Produced by fermentation of sugars/starch with yeast: C₆H₁₂O₆ →(yeast)→ 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂.
Board Tip: Methanol → formaldehyde → formic acid → blindness/death. Ethanol → safely metabolised in moderate quantities. Both toxic metabolites must be named. The fermentation equation for ethanol production is the standard 1-mark recall.

Case BasedPYQ 2015
Read the following and answer the questions:
A student was given three compounds — Compound A (C₄H₁₀), Compound B (C₄H₈), and Compound C (C₄H₆) — and asked to compare their properties. She found that only compound B decolourised bromine water but A and C did not react in the same way. On adding Br₂ in sunlight to A, a substitution product formed. She also noted that boiling points increased in the order A < B < C for the same number of carbons.
(i) Identify A, B, and C. Name the homologous series each belongs to.
(ii) Why does only B decolourise bromine water (not A)?
(iii) Write the equation for the reaction of A with Cl₂ in sunlight.
(iv) Why does boiling point increase with increasing unsaturation (from A to C)?
▼
✅ Answer
(i) A = Butane (C₄H₁₀) — alkane (CₙH₂ₙ₊₂, saturated). B = Butene (C₄H₈) — alkene (CₙH₂ₙ, one C=C). C = Butyne (C₄H₆) — alkyne (CₙH₂ₙ₋₂, one C≡C).

(ii) B decolourises, A does not: B (butene) is unsaturated — has C=C double bond → undergoes addition with Br₂: C₄H₈ + Br₂ → C₄H₈Br₂ (decolourises). A (butane) is saturated — no π bond → cannot undergo addition with Br₂(aq).

(iii) A + Cl₂ in sunlight:
C₄H₁₀ + Cl₂ →(sunlight)→ C₄H₉Cl + HCl
(Substitution — one H replaced by Cl)

(iv) Boiling point trend: As unsaturation increases (single → double → triple bond), the molecular mass stays similar but molecular shape becomes more linear and rigid. Increased surface contact area and stronger intermolecular forces → higher boiling point. Also, more compact packing possible → stronger van der Waals interactions.
Board Tip: Formula identification: C₄H₁₀ (2n+2 formula) = alkane. C₄H₈ (2n formula) = alkene. C₄H₆ (2n-2 formula) = alkyne. Recognise the series by the formula pattern — this is a fundamental skill tested in MCQ, VSA, and case questions throughout the chapter.
